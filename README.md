# kelp-3d-experiments

A collection of standalone 3D visualisation experiments.

## Projects

### [solar-visualiser](solar-visualiser/)

A browser-based 3D viewer for solar panel site surveys, built with [three.js](https://threejs.org/). Point it at a property and it renders the terrain, roof, and a proposed panel layout so you can sanity-check a survey without visiting the site.

**Running it:** it's a static site with no build step — just open `solar-visualiser/index.html` in a browser (`file://` works fine — all imagery is embedded as data URLs precisely so it does), or serve the folder with any static file server.

**What you see:**
- A 3D scene of the property: terrain from a digital surface model, draped with the actual aerial photo, plus the roof faces, solar panels, and any obstructions (chimneys, vents, etc.)
- A sidebar with site summary stats — suitability outcome, confidence, storeys, ridge height, panel count, estimated annual output — and a legend colour-coding roof faces by orientation (N/S/E/W)
- Toggles to show/hide each layer (terrain, aerial image, roofs, panels, obstructions, rejected roof faces, wireframe terrain), plus sliders for vertical exaggeration, image/roof opacity, and panel mounting height
- A **scaffolding designer**: draw a scaffold run around the building in plan or 3D view, and it auto-generates the scaffold geometry and estimates cost from length × height × a configurable £/m² rate
- A **mode toggle** at the top switches between **Solar** (survey, panels, scaffolding), **ASHP** (the building model below), and **EVC** (placeholder, coming soon)
- A **building model** (ASHP mode): turns the recognised roof faces into a regularised, watertight solid (vertices snapped to roof-plane intersections, footprint squared up, walls extruded to ground), slices it into floors with configurable storey height and slab thickness, lets you partition each floor into rooms by drawing dividing walls in plan view, and place windows (real holes with glass) and radiators on the walls. Edits persist locally and can be exported/imported as JSON — the same format a traced estate-agent floorplan will eventually feed into
- **Property images** (ASHP mode): the listing photos and floorplan in `data/images/` are described by an AI-generated manifest (`data/image-data.js` — classification, room guesses, floorplan room rectangles with printed dimensions, plus a structured read of the agent's description: window sides and radiator counts per room; suggestions only, produced offline). A photo gallery shows them in the sidebar, and **Import rooms from floorplan** fits the plan onto a floor (scale from the printed dimensions, orientation + translation from an outline fit, nudge buttons to correct), converts the plan's rooms into ordinary dividing walls, and labels/types the resulting rooms — one click from empty floor to a named, editable room layout
- **Editable, parametric model**: imported walls stay square to the building axes and every wall is directly editable — click to select, drag sideways to move (attached walls follow), delete outright — with the floorplan still visible as a toggleable underlay. The roof-derived footprint itself is editable too: drag its corner handles (edges snap square) or click one and delete it to remove a sliver segment, and the change flows downstream — walls re-extrude, floors re-slice, rooms re-snap, windows rebind — with edits persisted separately so the derived model can always be reset. Collinear wall runs are merged, so a gable end is one planar wall from eave corner to eave corner (apex and steps become its top profile) rather than several slivers a window couldn't span
- **Manual roof editing** (ASHP → Roof Geometry): trace and redraw roof faces over the aerial, drag or numerically position corners, set heights and pitch, split dormers into separate faces, and add missing extension roofs. Refit heights and pitch to the original DSM while keeping traced outlines and joined ridges. Check an orbitable 3D preview with DSM sample points and enlarge listing photos/floorplans alongside the plan. Drafts have undo/redo and cancel; Apply saves property-specific corrections, validates closed geometry and flags existing photo matches for review.
- **Broom Road reconstruction experiment**: compare a floorplan-based hypothesis with photo/DSM refinements, inspect projected windows and doors across four front photos, and review the corrected pair of mono-pitch rear roofs. Open `solar-visualiser/reconstruction.html` or use the Broom Road ASHP sidebar. The experiment preserves source data and saved edits; inferred details and the unrun depth stage are labelled. Rear photo overlays expose failed checks; OS outlines and attribute provenance are included. `solar-visualiser/survey.html` adds room/emitter evidence, survey input and homeowner options. See [the reproducible workflow](solar-visualiser/reconstruction/WORKFLOW.md) for the path to a reviewed heat-pump design.
- **Rooms & envelope preparation** (ASHP mode): adopt the refined exterior, divide floors into rooms, estimate internal areas/heights/volumes and classify external, party, internal, ground and unheated boundaries. Save a remote snapshot, then use a prioritised site-check list to record measurements with evidence and explicitly accept corrections. Unchecked fields remain remote; history survives model restoration. A listing-plan inventory provides rough estimates for all twelve Broom rooms, including the unmodeled basement. Calculations are deferred. See [the workflow guide](solar-visualiser/reconstruction/ASHP-WORKBENCH.md).
- **Photo positions** (ASHP mode): each exterior photo's camera pose is recovered from 2D↔3D correspondences between AI-suggested landmark dots and named corners of the solid. The match panel re-projects the model's wireframe live over the photo while you drag the dots — you trust the pose when the wireframe locks onto the brickwork. When the suggestions are bad, "Align view" lets you orbit the 3D view behind the semi-transparent photo until the model lines up — that camera becomes the pose. Accepted photos appear in the scene as camera frustums with thumbnails; "Stand here" flies the camera to the photographer's spot and cross-fades the photo over the model at the solved focal length
- **Facade projection**: with matched photos, "Project photos onto walls" bakes them onto the wall panels — every texel back-projected through the poses with occlusion handling, so the model wears the real brickwork to scale. Click a wall to open the rectified facade editor and drag rectangles over the visible windows and doors: the photo does the measuring, a Window/Door chooser pops up right beside each rectangle, and each becomes a real opening (hole + glass) in the model. Per-wall ▲/▼ buttons nudge the projected photo up or down a few centimetres when roof or ground bleeds onto a facade
- Mouse controls: drag to rotate, shift+drag to pan, scroll to zoom, plus a compass for orientation and hover tooltips on roof faces/panels

The **Property** selector switches between the original PO19 5DZ survey and **3 Broom Road · WA15 9AR**. Open `solar-visualiser/index.html?property=3broomroad` for the new address directly. Summary values, terrain metadata, photos and camera placement follow the selected property; building edits are saved separately per address. Data remains bundled for offline use, with a repeatable preparation script for the new raw exports. See [the Broom Road data guide](solar-visualiser/3broomroad-data/README.md) for regeneration and model coverage (the basement and full rear extension are not represented by the solar survey). See [solar-visualiser/CLAUDE.md](solar-visualiser/CLAUDE.md) for the module layout if you're diving into the code, and [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md) for the operational knowledge (tests, verification harness, gotchas).

### Blender / IFC drawing pipeline (repo root)

The regularised building solid also feeds a BIM workflow driven from Blender with the [Bonsai](https://bonsaibim.org/) (BlenderBIM) add-on:

- `solar-visualiser/data/rebuild-solid.json` — the wall-panel export of the web app's solid (panel plan segments, top profiles, eave/ridge heights)
- `rebuild_house_geometry.py` — run inside Blender: procedurally rebuilds the walls, gables and slabs in `model.blend` from that JSON (0.3 m wall thickness, both storeys, stepped walls)
- `sync_ifc_geometry.py` — pushes the rebuilt Blender meshes into the IFC representations (`model.ifc`), respecting each element's IFC placement
- `render_views.py` — regenerates the standard drawing set in `drawings/` (ground/first floor plans, all four elevations, isometric) via Bonsai drawing cameras, plus rendered views in `renders/`

A `blender-mcp` server is configured in `opencode.json` for driving Blender interactively from an agent.
